VINCENZO IANNONE
Psychotherapeutic Counsellor in London

I work with people who may be struggling with anxiety, low mood, relationship difficulties, loss, or a sense of being stuck in patterns that keep repeating.

Sometimes the difficulty is clear. At other times life may appear to be working, yet something still feels missing.

My approach is rooted in Psychosynthesis and in an understanding of psychological patterns not simply as problems to eliminate, but as responses that may once have had an important purpose.

When The Same Thing Keeps Happening

You may notice yourself pleasing other people even when it costs you, trying to keep everything under control, withdrawing when relationships become difficult, or being unable to enjoy something you have worked hard to achieve.

These experiences can look very different. But sometimes an old response continues because, at some point, it helped protect something important.

Understanding that function can change the question from “How do I get rid of this?” to “What has this been trying to do for me?”

A Different Relationship With The Pattern

Change does not always mean becoming the opposite of what you are.

Someone who has spent years pleasing others does not need to become uncaring. The same capacity for kindness can remain while no longer being driven by the fear of rejection.

The behaviour may look similar. What changes is where it comes from.

Psychosynthesis Counselling

Psychosynthesis offers a way of recognising that a painful feeling, reaction or protective part can be present without being the whole of who you are.

My work develops from this foundation, bringing together Psychosynthesis with an exploration of character, early wounds, the purpose of protective responses, and the possibility of becoming less identified with the patterns that have organised our lives.

Anxiety, relationship difficulties, loss, low self-esteem, repeating patterns, or simply the feeling that something in your life needs to change.

How I Work

How a response that once protected us can become a pattern — and what can happen when we no longer have to live entirely through it.

These experiences are not all the same, and not every form of suffering should be interpreted as a defence. Grief, loss and the realities of present life deserve to be met as they are.

But when something keeps repeating, another question can become useful:

Why does something I know is hurting me keep repeating?

A difficult experience can become a repeated pattern. A repeated pattern may have a function. And a response that causes difficulty today may once have helped protect something important.

HOW I WORK

Understanding What A Pattern Is Doing

When something in us causes pain, the natural reaction is often to want to stop it.

The anxiety. The need to control. The tendency to please. The inner critic. The impulse to withdraw.

But I am interested in another question as well:

What is this response trying to do?

A response that creates difficulty today may once have developed for a reason. It may have helped us maintain connection, avoid rejection, anticipate danger, preserve a sense of control, or find a way through an environment we could not change.

This does not mean that every difficulty is a defence, or that present suffering should always be explained through the past. It means that when a pattern keeps repeating, understanding its function can be as important as understanding where it came from.

The Wound And The Response

In my work I distinguish between two inseparable elements:

the wound — what happened to us or what was missing;

and

the response — what developed in order not to experience that wound again.

I call this structure the Binomio.

WOUND — where it comes from — ETIOLOGY
+
RESPONSE — what it is trying to prevent — TELEOLOGY
=
BINOMIO

The response is therefore not meaningless.

Someone who learned to please may be trying to prevent rejection.

Someone who needs to control may be trying never again to experience helplessness or uncertainty.

Someone who withdraws may be protecting themselves from a relationship that has come to feel dangerous.

The particular story is different for every person. The important point is that the response has a direction.

When The Protection Works

One reason these patterns can last for years is that they may actually work.

The person who pleases may be accepted.
The person who controls may prevent mistakes.
The person who anticipates every possible problem may become extremely competent.
The person who is hard on themselves may achieve a great deal.

And this creates an important difficulty:

success does not necessarily free us from the pattern. Sometimes it confirms it.

Imagine someone whose underlying fear is rejection.

When they are rejected, something inside says:
“I knew it. I am rejectable.”

But when they please someone and are accepted, the same structure can say:
“It worked. I was accepted because I pleased.”

One result feels painful and the other feels good.

But both can keep the same structure alive.

The Two Ends Of The Same Axis

I think of this as an axis.

ACCEPTED — because I pleased
│
│
REJECTED

The upper end is not necessarily healing.

It may simply be the successful end of the same strategy.

This matters because psychological change can otherwise become an attempt to move from one pole to its opposite:

rejected → accepted
weak → strong
dependent → independent
failure → success

But if both positions are organised around the same wound, changing poles does not necessarily change the underlying structure.

Change Is Not Becoming The Opposite

This is why I do not understand therapeutic change simply as replacing one personality with another.

The person who pleases does not need to learn to become uncaring.
The sensitive person does not need to become insensitive.
The person capable of anticipating difficulties does not need to lose that capacity.

Something subtler can happen.

The response can cease to have the job it once had.

The kindness can remain without having to purchase acceptance.
Care can remain without having to prevent abandonment.
Attention can remain without having to control everything.

The behaviour does not necessarily have to disappear. The necessity that produces it can disappear.

This is what, in my own theoretical language, I describe as the defensive function being exorcised.

Not the destruction of a quality.

Its liberation from an old mandate.

Disidentification

This connects with one of the central ideas of Psychosynthesis: disidentification.

A feeling can be present without being the whole of me.
A frightened part can be present.
An angry part can be present.
The need to please can appear.
The old reaction can even arrive before I have time to think.

But:

what is in me does not have to become the total definition of what I am.

In my own development of this idea, disidentification is not simply an exercise in standing back and observing ourselves.

It is a change in position.

The same person remains.
The history remains.
Often the capacities developed through that history remain.

But the defensive response no longer has to organise the whole person.

What Was There Before The Defence?

Here my work goes beyond asking only what went wrong.

Because there is another question:

What did the defence use?

A person who compulsively cares for everybody already possesses a capacity for care.
A person whose intelligence is continually used to anticipate danger already possesses intelligence.
A person who has spent a lifetime reading other people's moods may possess extraordinary sensitivity.

The wound did not necessarily create these capacities.

It may have requisitioned them.

That distinction is important in my work.

QUALITY → requisitioned by → DEFENSIVE PURPOSE → PATTERN

So therapeutic work is not necessarily about manufacturing a healthier person from damaged material.

Sometimes it is about recognising what was already there and seeing what job it was forced to perform.

The Same Quality, From Another Source

This leads to a distinction that has become increasingly important in my work.

Two actions can look identical from the outside and come from very different places.

I am kind because I need you not to reject me.

versus

I am kind because kindness is what I want to express.

Same person.

Perhaps even the same gesture.

But not the same psychological movement.

I use the word Trabocco for this second movement: expression that comes from what is already present rather than being organised primarily around what must be prevented or obtained.

And importantly, I do not understand this inner resource as something that suddenly appears after healing.

It was there while the person was anxious.
It was there while they were pleasing.
It was there while they were controlling.
It was there during the crisis.

The question is not simply whether we have inner resources.

The question is what they have been recruited to serve.

What This Means In Counselling

So when we work together, I am interested in more than removing a symptom.

We may explore:

where a pattern came from;
what it has been protecting;
what capacities it has recruited;
how it continues to organise your present life;
and what becomes possible when those capacities no longer have to perform the same defensive job.

The purpose is not to turn you into your opposite.

It is to create enough space for what has become automatic to be recognised — and for a different relationship with it to become possible.

The pattern can become something you have, rather than everything you are.

WHY DO I KEEP PLEASING PEOPLE?

When Being Kind Becomes Something You Have To Do

Caring about other people is not a problem.

Being attentive, generous or sensitive to what somebody else needs can be an important part of who you are.

The difficulty begins when kindness no longer feels entirely free.

You may notice that you say yes when you want to say no. You adapt quickly to other people. You sense disappointment before anything has been said. You try to prevent conflict. And sometimes you realise what you wanted only after the other person has gone.

From the outside, this can simply look like being a caring person.

Inside, something else may be happening:

being accepted has become connected with pleasing.

Why Does It Continue?

People pleasing is often described as a habit that needs to be stopped.

But there is another question worth asking:

What has pleasing been protecting you from?

For one person it may be rejection.
For another, anger.
For another, the possibility that somebody will leave.
For someone else, simply the feeling that they have disappointed another person may be extraordinarily difficult to tolerate.

The history is individual.

But when pleasing has developed as protection, it has a purpose:

Fear Of Rejection → Please → Preserve Acceptance

This is why simply telling yourself to “set better boundaries” may not reach the whole problem.

The behaviour makes sense inside the system that produced it.

When Pleasing Works

There is a more subtle problem.

People pleasing can work.

You accommodate somebody.
They are pleased.
The tension disappears.
You are accepted.
And you feel better.

It would seem that everything has gone well.

But psychologically something else can also have been learned:

“I was accepted because I pleased.”

The success of the strategy can therefore become evidence that the strategy was necessary.

Acceptance And Rejection Can Belong To The Same Pattern

Imagine a line with two ends.

ACCEPTED — “Pleasing worked.”
│
│
REJECTED — “The fear was right.”

Rejection hurts and appears to confirm the original fear.

Acceptance feels good, but when it is attributed to pleasing, it can confirm the strategy.

So the problem is not simply:

“How can I move from rejection to acceptance?”

Both can belong to the same psychological axis.

The positive pole is not necessarily freedom from the pattern.

This is why I do not see change as simply reaching the better end of the line.

The possibility is to become less organised by the line itself.

You Do Not Have To Become Less Caring

This distinction matters enormously.

If pleasing has become painful, the solution is not necessarily to become less kind.

Because kindness may never have been the problem.

Something valuable may have been recruited into a defensive task.

CAPACITY FOR CARE → fear of rejection → “I must use this to remain accepted.” → PEOPLE PLEASING

In my theoretical language I call this requisition.

The wound does not have to create the quality it uses.

It can take something already present — sensitivity, intelligence, care, attentiveness — and give it a defensive job.

The Wound And The Response

I call the relationship between a wound and the response organised around it the Binomio.

FERITA — fear / experience of rejection
+
RISPOSTA — pleasing in order not to be rejected again
=
BINOMIO

The first side asks:

Where did this come from?

The second asks:

What is this trying to prevent now?

Both matter.

Understanding only the past can leave the present strategy untouched.

Trying only to change the present behaviour can ignore why the strategy became necessary.

What Happens When The Strategy Is No Longer Necessary?

Something very interesting becomes possible.

You may still be kind.
You may still notice what another person needs.
You may still enjoy giving.

But the old transaction can begin to disappear.

Before:

I GIVE → so that you accept me → so that I do not experience rejection

After:

I GIVE → because I want to give

The outward action may be almost identical.

Its teleology has changed.

The response has lost its defensive mandate.

This is what I mean when I describe the defensive function as being exorcised.

The kindness has not been destroyed.

It has been released from the job of keeping you safe.

The Same Kindness, From Another Source

This distinction leads to something deeper in my work.

I do not think that the energy to care suddenly appears when the psychological problem is solved.

It was already there.

It was there even while it was being used to please.

I use the word Trabocco for the movement of what is already present into expression.

KINDNESS IS PRESENT → recruited by fear → people pleasing
recognition
KINDNESS IS PRESENT → no longer recruited by the same fear → freer expression

The capital did not disappear and return.

Its use changed.

Counselling For People Pleasing

In counselling, then, I would not begin by treating your caring nature as the enemy.

We can become curious about the whole movement:

where the fear of rejection came from;
how pleasing became connected with safety or acceptance;
what happens when somebody is disappointed with you;
what you imagine might happen if you stopped pleasing;
and what remains when care no longer has to secure your place with another person.

The aim is not to teach you to care less.

It is to explore whether care can become something you give, rather than something you have to pay.

WHY CAN'T I ENJOY WHAT I'VE ACHIEVED?

When The Facts Say One Thing And Something Inside Says Another

You worked for something.

Perhaps for years.

You finally achieved it.

For a moment there may be relief, satisfaction or even pride.

Then something happens.

The achievement becomes smaller.

You notice what could have been better.
You compare yourself with somebody else.
You tell yourself you were lucky.
Or your attention immediately moves to the next thing that needs to be done.

The achievement is real.

But somehow it does not quite become yours.

When The Problem Is Not The Fact

This interests me because sometimes there is nothing wrong with the evidence.

You did the work.
You built the relationship.
You completed the qualification.
You survived the difficult period.
You created something.

The facts are there.

What changes is the attribution.

FACT: “I did this.”
↓
ATTRIBUTION:
“It was luck.”
“Anyone could have done it.”
“It isn't enough.”
“I had help.”
“It doesn't really count.”

The fact survives.

The authorship moves.

This is one of the structures I have become particularly interested in:

sometimes we do not falsify what happened. We falsify the signature underneath it.

The False Signature

I call this the False Signature.

Imagine that your life contains an archive.

The evidence enters correctly:

I studied.
I worked.
I created.
I endured.
I chose.
I changed.

But before the record is closed, something alters the signature.

I DID IT → YES, BUT... → luck / circumstances / other people / not enough / anyone could → AUTHOR REMOVED

This can create a peculiar form of suffering.

You may possess the result without being able fully to inhabit the fact that you are one of its causes.

The Inner Tribunal

Behind the false signature there can be another movement.

A kind of internal tribunal.

It asks questions such as:

Am I really entitled to say this is mine?
Have I done enough to deserve it?
Who am I to take credit?
Am I allowed to feel proud?
What will happen if I take my place fully?

The tribunal appears to have authority.

So we defend ourselves before it.

We explain.
We minimise.
We work harder.
We ask for reassurance.
We wait for somebody else to certify what the facts already show.

And sometimes the work never ends because every new achievement simply becomes another case for the tribunal.

What If The Tribunal Has No Jurisdiction?

There is a radical possibility here.

Perhaps the solution is not finally obtaining the correct verdict.

Perhaps the tribunal was never entitled to judge the case.

“I need permission.” → WHO MUST GIVE IT?
“I need to deserve this.” → WHO HOLDS THE ACCOUNT?
“I need to prove that I am entitled.” → TO WHOM?

The question changes.

Instead of:

“How do I finally win my case?”

it becomes:

“Why am I still living as though this court has jurisdiction over my existence?”

This is why, in my own language, the movement is not simply toward a more positive judgment.

It is toward defect of jurisdiction.

The court loses the case because the court never possessed the authority it claimed.

Mi Firmo

“Mi firmo” does not mean:

I did everything alone.

Nor:

Everything I do is wonderful.

Nor:

Nobody helped me.

It means something much simpler.

I stop removing myself from the facts of my own life.

Other people contributed.
Circumstances mattered.
Chance exists.
History matters.
And I was also there.

The Fact + My Participation = I Sign

The signature does not inflate the fact.

It stops falsifying its authorship.

Why Success May Not Solve The Problem

This connects directly with the axis described in my way of working.

Achievement can become another North pole.

You finally obtain what you believed would settle the question.

But if the achievement is still processed by the same internal tribunal, the result is absorbed without changing the underlying structure.

More Achievement ≠ More Freedom

Sometimes it simply produces more material for the archive.

This helps explain why a person can be objectively successful and still feel that the decisive proof has not yet arrived.

Counselling And Authorship

In counselling, I am interested in the gap between the life that has actually been lived and the way authorship of that life has been recorded.

We can explore what happens when you succeed.
What happens when somebody praises you.
What you immediately subtract.
Whose permission seems to be missing.
What guilt appears when you take credit.
And what the internal tribunal appears to be protecting you from.

The aim is not to persuade you that everything you do is exceptional.

It is more exact than that.

To allow the facts to remain facts — including the fact that you were there.

WHEN LIFE WORKS BUT SOMETHING IS MISSING

When There Is No Obvious Disaster

Sometimes people come to counselling because something has clearly happened.

A relationship has ended.
Anxiety has become overwhelming.
There has been a loss.
Work has become impossible.

But sometimes the situation is harder to name.

Your life may function.

You work.
You manage responsibilities.
Perhaps you have relationships, a home, achievements, plans.

Nothing obvious explains the feeling.

And yet:

something is missing.

When Getting What You Wanted Does Not Settle The Question

We often imagine dissatisfaction as evidence that something has gone wrong.

But sometimes dissatisfaction becomes especially visible when things have gone right.

You reached the goal.
The feared catastrophe did not happen.
You became competent.
Independent.
Needed.
Successful.
Accepted.

And still there is a question underneath it.

Is this all?

This does not automatically mean the life you built was false.

It may mean that a life can become very successful at solving one problem without answering another.

The Successful Pole

A protective strategy can fail.

But it can also succeed.

Someone who learned to control may build an extraordinarily organised life.
Someone who learned to achieve may become highly successful.
Someone who learned to be indispensable may become needed everywhere.
Someone who learned to adapt may become excellent at fitting into almost any environment.

These are real capacities.

But if their primary psychological task has been to keep us away from an old feared position, reaching the successful pole does not necessarily end the system.

WHAT I FEARED → protective movement → WHAT I ACHIEVED

The entire journey can still belong to the same axis.

A Different Question

At this point another question becomes possible.

Not:

What else do I need to obtain?

But:

What in me wants expression?

That is a very different orientation.

One begins with lack:

I NEED X → I obtain X → perhaps I will finally be complete

The other begins with something already present:

SOMETHING IS HERE → it seeks form → expression

I use the word Trabocco for this second movement.

Trabocco

Trabocco means overflow.

But I do not use it to describe permanent happiness, positivity or a special spiritual state.

The underlying capital is present whether life feels good or terrible.

It is present in creativity.
It is present in anxiety.
It is present in love.
It can even be recruited into defensive patterns.

The sun illuminates the small bird eating and the wolf killing the lamb.

The light itself does not decide what it illuminates.

In the same way, I think of the Raggio as a source that does not disappear when our lives become painful.

And the Trabocco as a capital that does not suddenly begin only after psychological healing.

It overflows anyway.

The question is how that capital is being organised and used.

The Capital Does Not Need To Be Manufactured

This changes the therapeutic question.

Instead of:

How can I manufacture the missing quality?

we can sometimes ask:

Where is the quality already present, and what has it been recruited to do?

The person who feels exhausted from caring may possess enormous care.
The controlling person may possess attention, foresight and intelligence.
The person driven by achievement may possess extraordinary creative force.

The problem is not necessarily the capital.

It can be the route.

SAME CAPITAL → defensive route
SAME CAPITAL → expressive route

Not Another Perfect Self

This is also why I am cautious about turning psychological or spiritual development into another project of self-improvement.

The person can spend years trying to become:

calmer,
more spiritual,
more independent,
more successful,
more healed.

And the project can quietly become another North pole.

The form has changed.

The axis has not.

The question is not necessarily:

“What superior version of myself should I become?”

It may be:

“What becomes possible when I no longer need to organise so much of my life around avoiding one old position?”

The Monaco Laico

I use the expression Monaco Laico — the Secular Monk — for a particular position of the self.

Not somebody who leaves ordinary life.

The opposite.

Someone who returns to it.

Relationships remain.
Work remains.
Bills remain.
The body remains.
Conflict remains.
Loss remains.
The village remains.

What changes is the position from which the person inhabits them.

The Monaco Laico can know the silence of the mountain without requiring the village to become silent.

This matters to me because a psychological or spiritual insight that can survive only by withdrawing from ordinary life is incomplete.

The movement has to return to the real.

Something Missing — Or Something Waiting?

So the experience:

“My life works, but something is missing”

can sometimes be approached from another direction.

Perhaps the question is not only what is absent.

Perhaps there is also something present that has not yet found its form.

NOT ONLY:
“What am I missing?”

BUT ALSO:
“What in me has not yet found a way to live?”

Counselling can become a place in which that distinction is explored.

Not by abandoning the life you have built.

But by discovering whether more of you can inhabit it.

ABOUT VINCENZO / ROOTS

Vincenzo Iannone

I am a BACP Registered Psychotherapeutic Counsellor working in London and online, in English and Italian.

I trained in Psychosynthesis and have worked with people since 2013.

My way of working remains rooted in Psychosynthesis while also drawing from many years of study of character, trauma, philosophy and the different ways human beings organise themselves around suffering.

Psychosynthesis

Psychosynthesis gave me one of the foundations of my work:

we can have many different parts without any one of those parts being the whole of who we are.

Fear can speak.
Anger can speak.
The critic can speak.
The part that wants approval can speak.
The part that wants to disappear can speak.

Psychosynthesis gave me a language for working with these different movements without reducing the person to any one of them.

It also gave me the fundamental movement of disidentification.

My own work has continued from there.

Character And Protection

Another important influence has been the study of character.

Character is not interesting to me merely as a way of classifying people.

I am interested in how a way of being became necessary.

A response can solve something.

It can preserve connection.
Reduce danger.
Maintain control.
Protect against rejection.

And precisely because it works, it can become increasingly difficult to recognise as a response rather than simply:

“This is who I am.”

This became one of the foundations of what I now call the Binomio: Ferita + Risposta — Wound + Response.

From Cause To Purpose

Psychology often asks an essential question:

Where did this come from?

My work keeps that question.

But it places another beside it:

What is it doing now?

The first is etiological.

The second is teleological.

Understanding the history of a pattern matters.

Understanding the purpose it continues to serve matters too.

This is one of the places where my study of psychology and philosophy have increasingly converged.

Intellectual Roots

My thinking has been shaped particularly by the tradition of Roberto Assagioli and Psychosynthesis, alongside the study of character, developmental wounds and trauma.

It has also been shaped by philosophy.

Thinkers including Plotinus, Spinoza, Kant, Kierkegaard, Schopenhauer, Heidegger and Sartre have contributed to the questions behind my work: what it means to exist, how experience becomes organised, what freedom can mean, and what remains when the identities through which we ordinarily understand ourselves begin to loosen.

I do not treat these thinkers as authorities whose ideas must simply be repeated.

They are part of the genealogy from which my own questions developed.

The Work That Emerged

Over time, several concepts began to connect.

The Binomio describes the relationship between wound and protective response.

Requisition describes how a pre-existing capacity can be recruited into defensive service.

The Axis describes how both the painful and apparently successful pole can maintain the same structure.

The False Signature describes the distortion of authorship when the facts of a life remain but the person continually removes themselves from those facts.

Raggio and Trabocco describe the underlying capital that remains present through both defensive and freer forms of expression.

And the Monaco Laico describes an I that can recognise these structures without needing to leave ordinary human life behind.

They are not separate techniques.

They are different views into the same question:

What belongs to the wound, what belongs to the response, and what belongs to the person before either became confused with the whole?

Counselling

The theoretical structure matters to me because there is a person sitting in front of me.

Someone may arrive because a relationship ended.
Because anxiety will not switch off.
Because they keep pleasing everyone.
Because they are exhausted from controlling everything.
Because grief has changed the shape of their life.
Because they have achieved what they wanted and cannot understand why it does not feel like enough.
Or simply because something is no longer working.

We begin there.

Not with a theory imposed on the person.

With what is actually happening.

The theory has value only insofar as it helps us see more clearly.

Working With Me

I offer counselling in English and Italian, in London and online.

My work can include talking therapy alongside mindfulness, imagery, work with different parts or subpersonalities, dreams, drawing and other experiential approaches when appropriate.

The work is collaborative and develops according to the person rather than requiring the person to fit a predetermined technique.

I also offer an initial consultation for people who would like to speak with me before deciding whether to begin counselling.
